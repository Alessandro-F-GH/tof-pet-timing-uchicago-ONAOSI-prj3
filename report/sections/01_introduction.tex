\section{Introduction}
Positron emission tomography (PET) detects pairs of annihilation photons to reconstruct
radiotracer distributions. Time-of-flight (TOF) information constrains the annihilation position
along the line joining the detectors and can improve image signal-to-noise performance.
The precision of this information depends on the coincidence timing resolution (CTR),
conventionally expressed as the full width at half maximum (FWHM) of the time-difference
distribution~\cite{surti2015}. Improving the extraction of timing information from detector
signals is therefore relevant in addition to improvements in scintillators, photodetectors
and front-end electronics.

A leading-edge discriminator (LED) assigns an arrival time when a pulse crosses a fixed
amplitude threshold. Its response depends on the pulse amplitude and rising-edge shape.
Digitized waveforms retain information that a single threshold crossing does not explicitly
use. Machine learning (ML) provides a means of combining this information in a timing estimator.
Berg and Cherry demonstrated TOF estimation from pairs of detector waveforms using a
convolutional neural network (CNN), with training labels obtained from controlled source
positions~\cite{berg2018}. Onishi et al. instead trained a network to correct the LED
time-difference error using waveforms acquired at one source position~\cite{onishi2022}.
This correction-based formulation separates the conventional timing measurement from the
waveform-dependent adjustment and motivates the approach considered here.

Subsequent work has explored richer waveform representations. Feng et al. combined
convolutional features with transformer-based attention to capture local and global temporal
information~\cite{feng2024}. For bismuth germanate (BGO) detectors, Loignon-Houle et al.
compared leading-edge timing, a two-threshold time-walk correction and a CNN-based method,
including their effects on the tails of the timing distribution~\cite{loignon2025}.
These studies motivate examining both the information used by a model and the definition of
timing resolution. Their detector materials and acquisition conditions differ from those
of the present study; their numerical performance is not used as a benchmark for our boards.

The present analysis compares waveform-based corrections with a common LED reference.
A detector-shared formulation applies the same scorer to each pulse and subtracts its outputs,
thereby imposing detector-exchange antisymmetry. Direct estimators provide alternatives
without this constraint. Linear regression, multilayer neural networks and convolutional
feature transforms are considered with short and extended waveform windows.
Two front-end boards are analysed independently. The objectives are to evaluate model and
window dependence within each board and to identify temporal regions to which the fitted
corrections are sensitive. No performance outcome is assumed in this draft.
