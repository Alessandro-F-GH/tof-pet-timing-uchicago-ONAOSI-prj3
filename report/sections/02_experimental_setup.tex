\section{Experimental setup and datasets}

\subsection{Detector modules and acquisition}

Two opposing detector modules consist of silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs)
coupled to $2\times2\times3\,\mathrm{mm}^{3}$ lutetium--yttrium
oxyorthosilicate (LYSO) crystals, with a $^{22}\mathrm{Na}$ source
positioned centrally between them. The University of Chicago (UC)
and Fondazione Bruno Kessler (FBK) configurations use the same
crystals and SiPMs but different analog front-end electronics.

The two-stage readout chain is shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:amplification-stages}. Its first stage produces a
relatively long signal suitable for energy estimation and conventional
timing. The second provides additional amplification and a sharper
signal optimized for timing. This dedicated stage requires a separate
5 V supply, adding hardware and integration challenges that make it
chalenging to deploy in clinical PET systems. The two outputs are referred to as
\emph{energy} and \emph{timing}, respectively.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figures/Amplifier_schema.png}
\caption{Two-stage readout chain. Energy and timing waveforms
are sampled from successive amplification stages of the same SiPM pulse.}
\label{fig:amplification-stages}
\end{figure}

The energy signal is acquired without amplitude clipping, and its
maximum is used for photopeak selection. For the high-gain timing
signal, the oscilloscope's narrower vertical range emphasizes the
rising edge while clipping the pulse peak, following a strategy
also used by Onishi et al.~\cite{onishi2022}. In these acquisitions,
the energy waveform is cleaner and more stable but exhibits greater
time walk; the timing waveform is more susceptible to noise and
pulse-shape variations.

\subsection{Acquisitions and scientific roles}

Each coincidence event contains four simultaneous waveforms:
first-stage outputs from detectors 1 and 2 on channels 1 and 2,
and second-stage outputs on channels 3 and 4. The $100\,\mathrm{ns}$
records are sampled at $80\,\mathrm{GS\,s}^{-1}$ with a SiPM bias
of $48\,\mathrm{V}$. The centrally located source defines a
true TOF of zero.

For each board, three independent acquisitions are used:
\emph{calibration} to determine preprocessing and LED selection criteria,
\emph{train} for model training and selection, and
\emph{test} for final evaluation. Each calibration dataset
contains approximately 20,000 raw coincidence events, while
the train and test sets contain approximately 100,000
events each, before event selection. The two boards are
analyzed independently.

Figures~\ref{fig:uc-raw-waveforms} and~\ref{fig:fbk-raw-waveforms}
show the energy and timing waveforms of the same illustrative
coincidence event for UC and FBK, respectively. Both detector
signals are shown for each amplification stage. These are raw
waveforms before event selection or preprocessing, with only the
acquisition vertical-range clipping applied.
