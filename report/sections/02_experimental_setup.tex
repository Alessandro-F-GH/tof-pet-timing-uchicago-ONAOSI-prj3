\section{Experimental setup and datasets}
\subsection{Detector modules and acquisition}
Two opposing detector modules comprise silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs) coupled to
lutetium--yttrium oxyorthosilicate (LYSO) crystals of dimensions
$2\times2\times3\,\mathrm{mm}^{3}$. A $^{22}\mathrm{Na}$ source is positioned centrally
between the modules. The University of Chicago (UC) and Fondazione Bruno Kessler (FBK)
measurements use the same crystals and SiPMs; the two arrangements differ in their
front-end electronics. Each board provides an energy output from the first amplification
stage and a timing output from a second stage that increases the gain to improve
sensitivity to pulse arrival.

The energy output is recorded to retain the energy information required for event
selection. Its complete pulse amplitude lies within the oscilloscope's vertical range,
and the maximum amplitude is used as the energy-selection observable. This first-stage
output represents the standard signal available for energy measurement in clinical
systems. The additional high-gain timing stage used here is costly and is not part of
standard clinical readout, motivating the application of LED and ML timing methods to
the energy signal as well. Timing analysis of waveforms that also provide energy
information has been investigated in previous work~\cite{berg2018}.

The two outputs have different signal characteristics. The energy waveform is cleaner
and more stable, but its leading-edge timing is more affected by time walk. The higher
gain of the timing output makes it more susceptible to noise and waveform instability.
For this output, the oscilloscope vertical range is deliberately much smaller than the
full pulse amplitude. This setting emphasizes the initial rising edge, while the pulse
peak is clipped in amplitude. It provides a timing-oriented acquisition intended to
approach the best timing resolution attainable with the same crystal and SiPM.
A narrow vertical range emphasizing pulse arrival has also been used in the waveform
acquisitions of Onishi et al.~\cite{onishi2022}.

Four oscilloscope channels record both modules: channels 1 and 2 contain the energy
waveforms, while channels 3 and 4 contain the timing waveforms. The documented acquisitions
use $100\,\mathrm{ns}$ records sampled at $80\,\mathrm{GS\,s}^{-1}$.
Each board is analysed independently, with energy-based event selection common to both
waveform modes. The energy mode examines timing information in the standard first-stage
signal; the timing mode examines the dedicated high-gain, narrow-range acquisition.

\subsection{Acquisitions and scientific roles}

$48\,\mathrm{V}$ for each board. They are assigned to control, development and blind roles,

Control determines the preprocessing and LED selection rules. Development provides all
