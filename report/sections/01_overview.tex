\section{Introduction and scientific scope}

\subsection{Motivation}

Previous waveform-based machine-learning studies have shown that learned models can improve timing in time-of-flight positron emission tomography (TOF-PET), including convolutional neural network (CNN)-based TOF estimation, hybrid Transformer--CNN approaches, and explicit machine-learning timing-correction methods.\footnote{E. Berg and S. R. Cherry, \emph{Using convolutional neural networks to estimate time-of-flight from PET detector waveforms}, \textit{Physics in Medicine \& Biology}, 2018; X. Feng, A. Muhashi, Y. Onishi, R. Ota, and H. Liu, \emph{Transformer-CNN hybrid network for improving PET time of flight prediction}, \textit{Physics in Medicine \& Biology}, 2024; S. Naunheim et al., \emph{Explicit machine-learning corrections and spatial generalization for PET detector timing}, \textit{Frontiers in Physics}, 2025.} This project builds on that result by asking what structure and what waveform information are responsible for the improvement.

Rather than just focusing on improving coincidence timing resolution (CTR), defined in Sec.~\ref{sec:ctr-estimator}, the study combines performance with physical constraints and interpretability. A detector-shared model is compared with a more general joint CNN, larger waveform windows are used to study where timing information is concentrated, and the same framework is tested on both the energy and dedicated timing signals.

\subsection{Project scope}

The first objective is to test a physically constrained representation of the detector pair. The proposed model applies the same function to both detector waveforms and forms the correction from the difference of the two outputs. This enforces detector-exchange antisymmetry and provides a natural detector-level representation. Its performance is compared with a joint CNN that processes the two waveforms together without this constraint.

The second objective is to investigate where useful timing information is located in the waveform. The analysis therefore considers larger input windows than the previous project baseline and combines timing performance with explainable AI (XAI) and, where possible, controlled window or ablation studies.

Finally, the method is evaluated on the two available analog signals. The energy channel is used as the main test bed for the architecture comparison and information-localization study. The same approach is then applied to the second-stage timing channel, which is specifically designed for timing and is expected to provide the best absolute CTR.

CTR remains the primary quantitative metric, considered together with model structure, temporal sensitivity, and consistency across the available datasets.

\subsection{Research questions}

The study is organized around three questions:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{Q\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Physical structure:} can the detector-shared correction introduced in Sec.~\ref{sec:shared-model} retain the timing performance of the more general joint model described in Sec.~\ref{sec:joint-model}?

    \item \textbf{Information localization:} which temporal regions of the waveform contribute useful information for timing correction?

    \item \textbf{Timing channel:} how does the same correction framework perform when applied to the dedicated timing waveform instead of the energy waveform?
\end{enumerate}
