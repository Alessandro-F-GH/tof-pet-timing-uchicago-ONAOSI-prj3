\section{Conclusions}
\subsection{CTR improvement}
Waveform-based correction substantially improved leading-edge 
timing on the first-stage energy signals of both boards. 
The test CTR was reduced by more than $20\%$ 
even with the short Onishi-inspired window, with reductions 
reaching approximately $25\%$ when the wider window was used. 
The strong performance of the
Onishi-inspired CNN in the short-window energy comparisons is
consistent with previous waveform-based LED correction
studies~\cite{onishi2022,loignon2025}.

\subsection{Shared detector-level models}
The detector-shared formulation retained essentially the same useful
timing correction as the direct formulation without using separate
detector-specific scoring functions. Across boards, waveform modes,
and windows, shared estimators generally remained in the same
performance range as the leading direct estimators. The small
few-picosecond differences between the best shared and direct results
are not interpreted as evidence that either formulation is intrinsically
superior, since such differences may depend on the detector and
acquisition setup. The main result is that most of the achievable
waveform-based correction can be retained with a shared detector-level
model.

\subsection{Model comparison}
CNN and MLP approaches did not provide a systematic advantage over
simpler estimators, despite the successful application of CNNs in
previous waveform-based timing studies~\cite{onishi2022,loignon2025}.
Across the tested configurations, linear ridge regression and
MiniRocket features followed by ridge regression reached the same
overall performance range as the neural models. Because the leading
estimators often differ by only a few picoseconds, their exact ordering
is not considered a general property of the model families and may
change with detector and acquisition conditions. The broader result is
that much of the waveform-dependent timing correction can be captured
without requiring end-to-end nonlinear neural networks. The marked FBK
energy-wide shared-CNN failure also highlights a practical advantage of
the ridge-based estimators: for fixed input features, ridge regression
has a convex objective and is not subject to the initialization-dependent
optimization of the neural models. Together with their competitive
timing performance, this makes the ridge-based approaches particularly
attractive for this correction task.

\subsection{Window length and XAI}
Extending the waveform window consistently improved
 the achievable timing resolution on both boards, 
 with a particularly important effect on the timing signals, 
 where short windows provided limited correction. 

 \textit{Preliminary XAI results. The temporal-sensitivity analysis is
still under validation and should be interpreted as exploratory.}
 Temporal occlusion analysis showed that energy-channel 
 models relied primarily on the region around the LED crossing,  
 while timing-channel models were also sensitive to later waveform structures. 
 This was especially evident for the FBK board, where a 
 pronounced secondary sensitivity peak appeared around $20\,\mathrm{ns}$. 
 These findings support the usefulness of waveform information 
 beyond the rising edge.

 \subsection{Final considerations}
 Despite ML correction, the second-stage timing signals achieved 
 approximately twice the timing resolution of the first-stage energy signals, 
 highlighting that waveform-based correction cannot fully compensate for differences 
 in analog readout quality. 
 Nevertheless, ML consistently improved LED timing, with both boards reaching similar 
 corrected CTRs of approximately $53$–$54\,\mathrm{ps}$ despite different amplification 
 electronics and LED thresholds. 
 This convergence suggests that the remaining timing uncertainty may be dominated by 
 physical effects common to the detectors that cannot be recovered from the available 
 waveform information, although this hypothesis requires further investigation.
